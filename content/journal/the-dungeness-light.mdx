Dungeness is a shingle headland the size of a small town, technically classified as one of the largest expanses of shingle in Europe, and often described — inaccurately but understandably — as Britain's only desert.

It is forty minutes from Rye and it does not look like anywhere else.

## What is there

Two lighthouses, one working. A nuclear power station, decommissioning. A miniature railway that arrives from Hythe. Perhaps thirty houses, several of them famous, scattered across the shingle without roads or fences or gardens in any conventional sense.

## The light

Photographers come for it. The combination of flat shingle, huge sky and no vertical features means the light does things here it does not do elsewhere — particularly in the hour before sunset and on overcast days when the whole landscape goes silver.

## Practicalities

There is a café. There is a pub. There is very little else, and no shelter of any kind. It is exposed in a way that is difficult to convey until you are standing in it.
