The canal was dug between 1804 and 1809 to stop Napoleon, on the reasoning that an invading army would struggle to cross a thirty-foot ditch under fire. It was never tested.

It runs twenty-eight miles from Seabrook to Cliff End, along the back of Romney Marsh, and the towpath is a public right of way for almost all of it.

## Why it is worth doing

It is completely flat. It is sheltered, which on this coast is rare. And it is empty — you can walk five miles on a Saturday in summer and pass four people.

The kinks in it are deliberate. Every few hundred yards the line changes angle so that guns positioned at the bend could fire along the straight.

## In sections

Nobody needs to do twenty-eight miles. The stretch from Appledore to Warehorne is about four miles, has a pub at each end, and is the best of it.
