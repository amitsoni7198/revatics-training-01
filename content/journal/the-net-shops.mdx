The net shops are three-storey wooden sheds, tarred black, standing on the shingle behind the boats. There is nothing else like them anywhere.

They are tall and narrow because ground rent on the Stade was charged by footprint. Building up rather than out was a tax dodge, and it produced one of the most distinctive pieces of vernacular architecture in England.

They were built to store nets, which rot if they are stored wet and folded. Hung full-length in a tarred shed with airflow, they last.

About forty survive. There were once several hundred.
