The Jurassic coast delivers fossils in a way that keeps most children going for about ninety minutes, which is roughly the length of a good beach trip.

## The rules

There are two and they are not negotiable.

1. Take only what is loose on the beach. Never hammer the cliff.
2. Never stand under an overhang, and never go close to the cliff after heavy rain.

Cliff falls here kill people. It is a working, actively eroding coastline, not a heritage site.

## When

Low tide, ideally within a day or two of a storm, when the sea has turned over the shingle and brought up new material. Winter is better than summer for this and considerably better for having the beach to yourself.

## What you will actually find

Ammonites, mostly small. Belemnites, which look like bullets and are far more common than children expect. Fragments of crinoid. Anything larger is a good day and worth showing somebody who knows.
