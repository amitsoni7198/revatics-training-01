Before there was a navy there was an arrangement. Five towns on this coast — Hastings, New Romney, Hythe, Dover and Sandwich — agreed to supply the Crown with ships and men for fifteen days a year. In return they got tax exemptions, self-government and the right to run their own courts.

Rye and Winchelsea joined later as Ancient Towns, which is a distinction nobody outside the confederation has ever cared about.

## Why it mattered

For about three hundred years these were among the most important towns in England. The wealth is still visible: Rye has a church and a castle out of all proportion to a town of four thousand people, because in 1350 it was not a town of four thousand people.

## Why it ended

The sea. Harbours silted, coastlines shifted, and by the sixteenth century most of the ports could not take a ship. Henry VIII built a standing navy at Portsmouth and the arrangement became ceremonial.

The titles survive. There is still a Lord Warden.
