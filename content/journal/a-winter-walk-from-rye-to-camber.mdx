The path leaves Rye at the bottom of Mermaid Street and follows the Rother out towards the sea. It is flat the entire way, which is unusual for this coast, and it is exposed the entire way, which is the trade-off.

## Getting to the start

Trains from Ashford run hourly and the walk begins four minutes from the station. If you are driving, the long-stay car park on Cinque Ports Street is the sensible option.

## The middle section

After the harbour the path narrows and runs along the top of the sea wall. On the left is the reserve; on the right, shingle, and then more shingle. In a westerly this is hard work and there is nowhere to shelter for about forty minutes.

Look for:

- Little terns on the shingle ridge between May and July
- Sea kale, which grows nowhere much else in England
- The remains of the 1940s pillboxes, slowly sliding seaward

## The end

Camber is a village of about a thousand people and a beach that gets a hundred times that on a hot Saturday. Out of season it is empty and enormous. The bus back to Rye runs roughly every half hour and takes fifteen minutes, which is the honest way to do this walk if the wind has beaten you.
