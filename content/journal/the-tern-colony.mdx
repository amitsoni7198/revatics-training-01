Little terns nest on open shingle, which means their nests are a scrape in the stones and effectively invisible. This is why the fenced areas on the reserve are fenced, and why the wardens get short with people who step over.

## When to go

The birds arrive in April and most have gone by early August. Late May and June are the peak — the colony is at its noisiest and the adults are fishing constantly, which is the thing worth watching.

## From where

The hide on the eastern path gives the best angle without disturbing anything. Take binoculars. There is a pair in each room at Thorne Barn and we would rather lend them than have people creeping closer.

Do not take a dog. Ground-nesting birds and dogs do not coexist, and a single loose dog can empty a section of the colony in an afternoon.
