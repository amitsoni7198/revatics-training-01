Every year at the collective's dinner this argument happens, and every year it is unresolved.

The candidates are always the same. The view east from the churchyard at Winchelsea, which takes in the entire level. The estuary from the top of the garden at Fern & Anchor. The shingle from the eastern hide at Rye Harbour on a clear evening.

## The case for Winchelsea

You are standing on what was a cliff and is now three miles inland. Everything between you and the sea is land the sea gave back — reclaimed marsh, sheep, drainage ditches in straight lines, and the light doing something remarkable across all of it.

It is free, it is always open, and there is a pub about ninety seconds away.

## The counter-argument

It is not the sea. Two of the six will not accept any view that does not have water in the foreground, and the argument stops there.
