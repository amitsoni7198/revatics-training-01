The barn is eighteenth century and was still in agricultural use until 1994. The conversion took the roof off, put insulation in, and put the same tiles back on. From the reserve path it looks exactly as it did.

It is very quiet here, in a way that people from cities sometimes find difficult for the first night. There is no through road. The nearest streetlight is a mile away, which is why the stargazing is worth the trip on its own in January.

## The reserve

Rye Harbour Nature Reserve begins at the end of the track. There are four hides, and in spring the tern colony on the shingle ridge is loud enough to hear from the barn.

There is a pair of binoculars in each room and a bird log in the hall that guests have been filling in since 2011. Somebody recorded a hoopoe in 2019 and nobody has ever been able to confirm it.

## No dogs

We are on the edge of a ground-nesting bird reserve and dogs are a problem here in a way they are not elsewhere. It is the one thing we will not bend on.
