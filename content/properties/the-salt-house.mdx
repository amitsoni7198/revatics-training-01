The Salt House was built in 1782 for a wool merchant who, by most accounts, was also running brandy up the Rother at night. The cellar still has the hatch he used. We have not opened it.

Ellen and Mark took the house on in 2009, when the roof leaked into three of the six bedrooms and the kitchen was a 1970s afterthought. It took four years. Most of the panelling is original; the parts that are not were cut and fitted by a joiner in Winchelsea who worked to the old profiles.

## The house

Six rooms across three floors, none of them the same shape. The staircase is steep and the ceilings are low in places — worth knowing if you are tall or travelling with anyone who finds stairs difficult. There is no lift and there cannot be one.

Breakfast is served in the panelled room at the front between eight and half past nine. Bread comes from the bakery on Lion Street, eggs from a smallholding at Playden.

## Getting here

Rye station is a nine-minute walk. Trains run to Ashford International, and from there to St Pancras in thirty-seven minutes. If you are driving, the parking is behind the house through the arch — it is tighter than it looks.
