The cottage was built for the coastguard in 1856 and the boathouse below it about twenty years later. They were separate properties until 1998, when the family that owned both knocked a gate through the wall between the gardens and stopped pretending otherwise.

Eleven rooms makes this the largest of the six, and the one that feels least like a guesthouse. People come for a week rather than a night. In August there are usually children on the slipway from breakfast until it gets dark.

## The slipway

It is ours, and guests can use it. If you are bringing a boat, tell us the length and draught when you book — the pontoon takes up to six metres and there is a waiting list in high summer.

There is a drying room off the back hall, which sounds like a small thing until it rains for four days.

## Access

The Slipway room is on the ground floor with step-free access from the car park and a wet room. The rest of the house is not accessible: the stairs are original, steep, and turn twice.
